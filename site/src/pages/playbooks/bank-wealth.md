## What they are

Bank wealth products (银行理财) are pooled investment products sold by banks, usually into
fixed-income and money-market assets. They are **not deposits**, and since the 2018 asset-management
rules they are **not guaranteed**. A "performance benchmark" is a target, not a promise.

The daily and short-term ones feel like a savings account with a slightly better rate. The
multi-year ones behave like bond funds with less transparency.

## The real gate is the risk questionnaire

Branches run a risk-tolerance questionnaire before selling anything. It is usually in Chinese, and
the result determines which products you are allowed to buy. If you cannot read it, you are guessing.

Ask for one of these, in this order:

1. A branch that has an English version (rare, but some do).
2. A banker willing to translate question by question, with you initialling each answer.
3. A family member or colleague who can sit with you — but make sure the answers are yours.

Do not let a banker fill it in for you. A mismatch between your answers and the product's risk level
is the fastest way to lose money you cannot afford to lose.

## What to ask before subscribing

- What is the product's risk level, and which level am I approved for?
- Is there a lock-up? Can I redeem early, and at what cost?
- What are all the fees, including the ones inside the product?
- What happens in the downside scenario — show me the worst historical quarter.
- Does this product require 双录 (audio-video recording of the sales conversation)? If so, when?

## The paperwork

- Passport and residence permit.
- Bank account (the product settles into it).
- Risk assessment result, kept for your records.
- Sometimes a second form for the 双录 process.

If the branch says wealth products are "for Chinese ID holders only", that is a branch-level policy,
not necessarily the bank's. Ask for the branch manager, or try another branch. Some banks do sell to
foreign passport holders; some genuinely do not.

## What could go wrong

| Risk | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Liquidity | Product runs three years; you need the money in year one |
| Credit | The underlying bonds default; the bank does not make you whole |
| Sales pressure | A "guaranteed" product that is actually a structured deposit or an insurance policy |
| Currency | You funded in foreign currency and the FX move wipes out the coupon |

## Getting out

Daily products settle T+1. Fixed-term products settle at maturity, or through a redemption window
that may only open once a quarter. Check the calendar before you buy, not after.

## The timeline, not the tax rate

An equity award has three taxable moments, and they are taxed differently:

| Moment | What happens | Typical treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Grant** | You are promised shares or options | Usually nothing yet |
| **Vest / exercise** | The shares become yours, or you exercise | Taxed as **wages**, often separately from salary |
| **Sale** | You sell the shares | Capital gain, with rules that differ by market and residency |

The mistake is treating the vest as a gift, and the sale as the only taxable event.

## Vest: the one that surprises people

For a listed employer, the discount or the value received at vest is treated as **employment income**, not
investment income. Two consequences:

1. **The employer withholds before you can sell.** On the vest date you may owe tax on shares you have
   not sold, and some plans sell a portion automatically to cover it.
2. **It is calculated separately from your salary.** Listed-company equity incentives use a separate
   calculation rather than being merged into comprehensive income — a rule that has been extended
   repeatedly and currently runs for several more years. Do not assume it is permanent.

For a **non-listed** employer, the treatment can be deferred: tax is postponed until you transfer the
shares, and then charged as a property transfer at a flat rate — but only if the plan has been filed
correctly. An unfiled plan loses the deferral and pushes the whole amount into the year of vesting.

## Sale: where residency matters again

Selling listed A-shares has long been **exempt from individual income tax** for individuals, while
selling shares of a non-listed company is taxed on the gain. But "individual" is doing a lot of work in
that sentence:

- If you are a Chinese tax resident, your worldwide gains are in scope, and your home country may also
  tax them.
- If you are not, only China-sourced income is in scope — and the sale of shares in a Chinese company is
  arguably China-sourced even if you trade from abroad.

This is the point where the [183-day question](/explainers/tax-residency-183-days) decides your bill.
Ask before you sell, not after.

## The paper you will need

- **Award agreement**, with the vesting schedule and the strike price if any.
- **Vesting statement** from the employer showing the value and the tax withheld.
- **Withholding certificate** — the document that lets you claim a foreign tax credit at home.
- **Cost basis record** for the eventual sale, especially if the shares vested over several years.

Without the vesting statement, your home-country return will treat the entire sale proceeds as gain.

## What to ask HR before you sign

1. Is the plan registered, and am I named in it as a foreign employee?
2. Which account do the shares sit in — mine, or a plan account?
3. What is withheld at vest, and does the plan sell shares to cover it?
4. What happens to unvested awards if I resign, or if my residence permit changes?
5. Who issues the Chinese tax certificate, and in whose name?

The answers decide whether the award is worth what the offer letter implies.

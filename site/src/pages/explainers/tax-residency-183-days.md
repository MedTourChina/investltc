## The test

You are a Chinese tax resident for a calendar year if you have a domicile in China, or if you live in
China for **183 days or more** in that year without a domicile. Days are counted by physical presence,
not by visa type, contract length or payroll location.

“Domicile” (住所) is not the same as “home”. It generally means a permanent residence status — household
registration, or habitual residence without leaving. Most foreign residents argue the 183-day test
instead.

## Why it matters

| If you are a tax resident | If you are not |
|---|---|
| Worldwide income is taxable in China, subject to treaty relief | Only China-sourced income is taxable |
| You file an annual reconciliation (年度汇算) | Employer withholding is usually the end of it |
| Foreign tax credits become relevant | Treaty and credit questions are narrower |
| You may need to declare foreign assets in some contexts | Your reporting is mostly home-country |

The status is about **tax**, not about immigration, banking or investment access. A person can be a tax
resident while holding a work permit, and a person on a work permit can fail the 183-day test in their
first year.

## The six-year rule

There is a second layer for foreign individuals without a domicile. If you are resident for **six
consecutive years**, each year meeting the 183-day test, and in none of those years do you leave China
for more than 30 days, your worldwide income becomes fully taxable in China, including income paid
abroad.

The counter is deliberate: a single absence of more than 30 days in a year resets the count. This is
why some long-term residents plan a longer trip every few years — and why the planning should be done
with an adviser, not from a blog post.

## Common mistakes

1. **Assuming the visa decides it.** A work permit does not make you a tax resident; days do.
2. **Counting only full days.** Entry and exit days generally count as days of presence.
3. **Ignoring the first and last year.** Partial years are where surprises happen.
4. **Forgetting the home country.** Being non-resident in China does not make you non-resident at home.
5. **Treating the 183-day test as a planning tool.** It is a test, not an election.

## What to keep

- Entry and exit records (the app or the stamps).
- Employment contract and start date.
- Payroll split between onshore and offshore pay.
- The tax residency self-certification you signed at the bank — it is the same question.

## What this page is not

It is not tax advice, and it does not decide your case. Treaties, days, domicile and income type
interact, and the answer differs by nationality. If the amount at stake is material, ask a licensed
adviser — and bring your day count with you.

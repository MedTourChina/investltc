## The shape of this path

A **foreign permanent resident identity card (外国人永久居留身份证)** — the card, not the passport, is
what you present. It carries a resident-style number, works as an identity document at banks,
brokers, hotels and transport counters, and removes most of the "we have never done this before"
friction that foreigners meet.

It does **not** change what you may invest in. That is the whole point of this page.

## What gets easier

| Step | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Bank account | Opened as an ordinary resident-style customer; fewer document requests |
| Securities account | Usually straightforward — the income question is easier to answer |
| Renewals | The card has a validity period, but no annual permit cycle |
| Online services | More systems accept the card number than accept a passport |
| Property and vehicles | The card works where a passport often needs a companion document |

## What does not change

- **The conversion quota.** The USD 50,000 annual convenience quota still applies to you as an
  individual. Permanent residence is a status, not a quota.
- **The capital account.** QFII, Northbound Stock Connect and CIBM Direct remain institutional
  routes. Nothing about the card opens them to you.
- **The product rules.** Risk grades, qualified-investor thresholds and fund subscription limits apply
  the same way.
- **Tax.** You are taxed on the same basis; permanent residence is not a tax status. Days in the
  country still decide residency.

<div class="callout callout--warn">
<p class="callout__title">"I have the card, so I can invest like a local"</p>
<p>Administratively, yes. Legally, no. The card removes document friction; it does not create
channels. Anyone who tells you otherwise is describing a workaround, not a rule.</p>
</div>

## The practical advantages that do exist

They are real, and they are all about friction rather than access:

1. **Fewer refusals.** The most common obstacle for foreigners is a counter that has never processed a
   passport, not a rule. The card removes that.
2. **Longer time horizons.** No annual renewal means you can hold a multi-year product without
   planning around a permit expiry.
3. **Better credit and service access.** Some banks offer resident-style products and limits to card
   holders.
4. **One identity document.** Keeping a single number across systems avoids the middle-name and
   transliteration mismatches that block refunds and transfers.

## The sequence, briefly

The [first thirty days](/start) still applies, with two changes:

- You can skip the "find a branch that has done this before" step.
- You can open the **securities account** in month one rather than month two, because the income
  question is easier to satisfy.

Everything after that — the risk assessment, the annual settlement, the housing fund if you are
employed — is unchanged.

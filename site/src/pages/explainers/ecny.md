## What it is

**e-CNY (数字人民币)** is the digital form of cash in circulation — M0 in the central bank's
classification. Not a deposit, not a payment-company balance, not a cryptocurrency. It is a claim on
the central bank, held in a wallet on your phone.

That one sentence explains everything else:

- **No interest.** Cash in a drawer does not earn interest, and neither does e-CNY.
- **Not a bank liability.** If a bank failed, your deposit would rely on deposit insurance; e-CNY
  would not need to, because it is not the bank's liability in the first place.
- **Not an investment.** There is no yield, no price and no market. Anyone offering to "invest" your
  e-CNY is selling something else.

## Why a foreigner might care

The useful part is the ordering:

> You can open a **basic wallet with your passport**, before you have a Chinese bank account.

For the first weeks after arriving, while the bank appointment is still pending, that is the
difference between paying for things and not. The catches arrive immediately afterwards: acceptance is
limited to pilot cities and participating merchants, and the basic wallet has low limits.

## The wallet tiers

Wallets are classified by how much identity you have provided, and the class sets the limits:

| Tier | What you provide | Rough limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Class IV | Phone number | Small balance cap, a few thousand a day |
| Class III | ID document, sometimes a bank card | A few tens of thousands balance, higher daily limit |
| Class II | Full identity plus a bank account, face check | High balance and daily limits, still capped |
| Class I | In person at a counter | Effectively uncapped |

Exact numbers differ by operator and change; the shape does not. **More identity, more capacity.**

## Where it works

- **Pilot cities only** — 26 of them, from Shenzhen and Suzhou to Shanghai, Chengdu and Hainan.
- **Participating merchants**, often through a sub-wallet inside an app you already use.
- **Not everywhere.** A restaurant that takes WeChat Pay does not automatically take e-CNY.

## The three confusions

**"It replaces my bank account."** No. It is a payment instrument. Salary, funds, wealth products and
the housing fund all still run through a bank.

**"It is traceable, so it is not private."** It is traceable to the central bank by design — that is
the point of a central-bank digital currency — with "controllable anonymity" for small amounts. If
privacy is your reason for using it, you have the wrong reason.

**"It is like a stablecoin."** No. A stablecoin is a private claim backed by reserves; e-CNY is
sovereign money. They are not the same asset class, and only one of them is legal tender.

## What to do with it

Treat it as **walking-around money**: a small balance for daily payments, opened early because it is
easy, then kept small. Do not park savings in it, because it earns nothing, and do not expect it to
replace the bank account you still need for everything else on this site.

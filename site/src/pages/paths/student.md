## The shape of this path

You arrive on an X1 or X2 visa and convert it to a **residence permit for study**. The university
handles much of the paperwork, often including a bank account opened on campus by a partner branch.

That is the easy half. The hard half is that almost every investment product asks for **proof of
income or employment**, which a student does not have.

## What you can do

| Step | Status | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SIM card | Yes | Passport, at a carrier branch |
| Bank account (Class I) | Yes | Often arranged by the university; check which class you were given |
| Alipay / WeChat Pay | Yes | Bind the domestic card |
| Fund account | Usually | Through the bank app; expect the conservative risk grade |
| Securities account | **Often refused** | Brokers commonly want employment or income proof |
| Housing fund / social insurance | No | No employment relationship |
| Annual tax settlement | Only if you earned | Scholarships and part-time work complicate this |

## The brokerage wall

This is the step that stops most students, and it is worth knowing before you queue:

- Many brokers require **proof of employment** or a local income record to open a securities account.
- Some accept a university certificate and a residence permit. It varies by broker and by city.
- Being refused at one branch is not a ruling about the whole country. It is one branch's policy.

Do not pay an intermediary to "solve" this. Unlicensed intermediation is the reliable way to lose
money, and a brokerage account opened through a false employment certificate is a much worse problem
than not having one.

## Part-time work

Study permits can be annotated to allow **part-time work or internships**, usually with the school's
consent and a notation on the permit. If you do work:

- The income is taxable, and it usually appears in the tax app under the employer.
- Enough months in the country may make you a **tax resident** for the year, which changes the
  filing position. Treaties often have a student article that changes it again — see
  [tax residency](/explainers/tax-residency-183-days).

## Money from home

Tuition and living costs arriving from your family are **current-account** transfers. The receiving
side is ordinary; keep the documents:

- The transfer purpose should say what it is (tuition, living costs).
- Your bank may ask for the admission letter or the tuition invoice on a large first transfer.
- Receiving money is not the problem. Sending it back out, or converting it to invest, is where the
  purpose rules apply.

## What to do in order

1. SIM card, then the bank account (accept the university's arrangement, but confirm it is Class I).
2. Bind the payment apps, and test a small transfer.
3. If you work part-time: keep every payslip and check the tax app shows the employer.
4. Open the **fund account** if you want to hold anything — it is the one that usually works.
5. Ask a broker about the securities account only after you have an employment or income record,
   which usually means after graduation.

## The honest summary

A student can bank, pay, receive money from home, and hold a money-market fund. A student generally
cannot trade. Plan around that rather than fighting it.

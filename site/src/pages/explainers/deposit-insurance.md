## The rule

China's deposit insurance scheme protects deposits up to **CNY 500,000 per depositor per bank**. If a
bank fails, the insurance fund pays out up to that amount, and the rest of the claim is settled through
the bank's liquidation.

The number is not indexed. It has been CNY 500,000 since the scheme started in 2015.

## What counts as a deposit

Insured deposits are the ordinary ones:

- Current and savings accounts
- Fixed-term deposits
- Notified and call deposits
- Deposits held by individuals and by legal entities

The protection attaches to **you as a depositor at that bank**, not to the bank group. Two banks under
one parent are usually two separate insured institutions, but confirm the legal entity rather than the
brand — branches of foreign banks in China are often separately incorporated.

## What does not count

This is where people lose money by assumption:

| Product | Insured? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bank wealth management (理财) | No | Not a deposit; it is a managed product with a risk grade |
| Structured deposits | Partly | Only the deposit component, and the terms are often opaque |
| Money-market funds | No | A fund, not a deposit |
| Bonds | No | An obligation of the issuer |
| Insurance savings products | No | Protected by insurance regulation, not deposit insurance |

Bank staff sometimes describe a wealth product as "like a deposit, but better". It is not, and the
difference is exactly this ceiling.

## What to do with it

If you hold more than CNY 500,000 in cash-equivalents:

1. **Split across banks.** Two banks give you CNY 1 million of cover, three give 1.5 million.
2. **Split across legal entities, not brands.** Check whether the second "bank" is a separate insured
   institution.
3. **Keep the excess in something you understand.** Bonds or funds are not insured either; the point is
   to know which risk you are taking, not to avoid all of them.

## The part that matters for foreigners

Deposit insurance applies to deposits held at insured institutions in China regardless of the
depositor's nationality. Your passport does not reduce the cover — but it also does not extend it, and
a deposit held through an offshore branch of a Chinese bank is a different claim.

If a bank fails, payout is organised through the deposit insurance fund and usually handled at the
branch. Keep your account documents and passbook or app records; reconstruction of a claim is much
easier with them.

## What it is

Chinese residents and foreign individuals with domestic accounts can convert foreign currency to RMB,
and RMB to foreign currency, within an annual **facilitation quota of USD 50,000 equivalent** per
person. Inside the quota, the bank processes the conversion on your declaration of a genuine
current-account purpose.

## What it covers

- Salary and bonuses received from abroad.
- Family support and living expenses.
- Proceeds from selling a personal asset, with documents.
- Tourism, education and medical expenses.
- Small current-account payments for services.

## What it does not cover

- **Buying securities.** That is a capital-account transaction.
- **Buying property for investment.** Property purchases have their own rules and approvals.
- **Moving an arbitrary large sum** by splitting it across people or banks.
- **Settling a business transaction** that should go through a company account.

<div class="callout callout--danger">
<p class="callout__title">Splitting is the classic violation</p>
<p>“Ant moving” (蚂蚁搬家) — dividing a large sum into sub-USD-50,000 transfers across family,
friends or banks — is explicitly targeted by regulators. So is declaring a purpose you know to be
false. Both can lead to account restrictions and personal liability.</p>
</div>

## How it interacts with tax residency

The quota is an FX rule, not a tax rule. Separately, spending 183 days or more in China in a calendar
year generally makes you a Chinese tax resident, which changes what income you must report. The two
tests are independent: passing one does not settle the other.

## The practical version

1. Confirm the **purpose** with your bank before sending anything.
2. Keep the supporting document for each transfer — contracts, invoices, sale agreements.
3. Do not split a transfer to stay below a threshold.
4. If you need more than the quota for a legitimate purpose, ask the bank about documentary review.
   Do not look for a workaround.

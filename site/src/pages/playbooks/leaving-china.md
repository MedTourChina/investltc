## The mistake everybody makes

People cancel the phone number first. Then they discover that every bank transfer, every broker
withdrawal and every app login needs an SMS code sent to that number. The account is still theirs;
they simply cannot prove it.

**Keep the Chinese number alive until the last transaction has settled.** It is the cheapest
insurance in this playbook.

## The order

Work backwards from the money leaving:

1. **Sell what you hold.** Funds and securities settle in one to three days; fixed deposits may need
   you to break them, and breaking early can cost the interest.
2. **Withdraw to your bank account.** One account, in your name, that you still control.
3. **Close the securities and fund accounts.** Empty first, then close — closing with a balance
   stranded inside is a return trip.
4. **Take the balances that need separate filings.** The housing fund and the social-insurance
   personal account each have their own form; see
   [housing fund and social insurance](/explainers/housing-fund-and-social-insurance).
5. **Remit or convert, with documents.** Salary and investment proceeds are current-account uses;
   keep the tax receipts and the source documents.
6. **Close the bank account last.** After everything else has cleared.
7. **Then cancel the phone number, the residence permit and the lease**, in that order.

## The remittance part

Money you earned in China leaves under the ordinary rules:

- **Purpose must be declared**, and the bank will ask for evidence. Salary needs the employment
  record and tax receipts; proceeds of an investment need the sale confirmation.
- **The USD 50,000 conversion quota** is a per-person, per-year limit on **conversion**, not on
  remitting your own money. Large amounts above it are possible with documents — that is a different
  process, not a forbidden one.
- **Splitting a transfer across friends or family is the thing to avoid.** It is the single most
  common way people turn an ordinary remittance into an investigation.

## Tax clearance

Ask the local tax office for a **tax clearance certificate (完税证明)** before you leave, not after.
Banks may ask for it on larger remittances, and your home country will want it for the foreign tax
credit. Getting it later, from abroad, is possible and slow.

If you are a Chinese tax resident for the year you leave, check whether a **departure filing** applies
and what the deadline is. Leaving mid-year does not always end the year's obligations on the day you
fly.

## What to keep, and for how long

| Document | Keep because |
| --- | --- |
| Tax receipts and clearance | Home-country credit, and bank requests |
| Sale confirmations | Cost basis, and proving the source of funds |
| Employment contract and payslips | Proving the money was earned, if asked later |
| Housing-fund withdrawal receipt | It is a separate balance, and it is easy to lose |
| Bank statements for the final months | The audit trail for the remittance |

Five years is a reasonable default; some jurisdictions expect longer for assets.

## If you are coming back

Do not close everything. A dormant account with a small balance and a live phone number is far
cheaper to reactivate than to reopen from abroad. Ask the bank what "dormant" means for them, and
whether a zero balance triggers closure.

## The one-page version

> Sell, withdraw, close the investment accounts, take the fund balances, remit with documents, close
> the bank, keep the phone until the last cent has landed, and take the tax clearance before you go.

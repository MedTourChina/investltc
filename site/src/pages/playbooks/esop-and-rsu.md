## Before you sign

The offer letter says "RSUs worth X". That number is a gross, unvested, pre-tax promise. Five questions
turn it into something you can compare:

1. **Is the plan registered?** A plan that exists for Chinese staff may not name foreign employees.
   Unregistered plans lose the tax deferral that makes them worth having.
2. **Which account do the shares go into?** Your own brokerage account, or a company-administered plan
   account you cannot trade freely from?
3. **What is the vesting schedule?** Four years with a one-year cliff is standard; anything longer is a
   retention tool, not compensation.
4. **What happens if you leave?** Unvested awards usually vanish; vested ones may have to be sold within
   a set window.
5. **Who handles the money?** If the employer sells shares and remits cash, the FX and tax handling is
   theirs. If you must do it, it is yours.

## On the vest date

This is the day the tax is triggered, and the day people are surprised.

- **Tax is withheld at vest**, based on the value on that date. The employer may sell part of the shares
  to cover it — check whether it does, because otherwise you must fund the tax from your salary.
- **The shares are yours net of tax**, but they may sit in a plan account. Ask when they can move to
  your own account, and what the transfer costs.
- **Keep the vesting statement.** It is the only document that proves your cost basis years later.

## Selling

If the shares are in a plan account, the sale window may be limited — a few weeks after vest, or a
company-designated trading period. Miss it and you wait a quarter.

If they are in your own account, the sale is an ordinary trade, with one exception: if you are no longer
a Chinese tax resident, the treatment of the gain changes, and it changes for your home country too.

## Getting the money out

This is where employee equity turns into a cross-border question:

1. **Proceeds from selling listed A-shares** stay in your onshore account in RMB. Moving them out follows
   the ordinary remittance rules — a permitted purpose, a declared purpose, and the
   [USD 50,000 conversion quota](/explainers/usd-50k-quota) where conversion is involved.
2. **Proceeds from a foreign parent's shares** are usually already offshore, and the question is whether
   the inflow was ever registered. Money that came in as a registered capital contribution can be
   repatriated on the same basis; money that came in informally cannot.
3. **The employer may handle the flow** through a dedicated plan account. Ask who does what before you
   need the money, not after.

None of these steps is a loophole. Each one is a documented route with a purpose requirement attached.

## The list to keep

- Award agreement and any amendment.
- Vesting statements, one per vest.
- Employer withholding certificates (完税证明).
- Sale confirmations, with dates and prices.
- Records of any transfer between the plan account and your own account.

If your home country gives a foreign tax credit, these five documents are the difference between a
credit and an argument.

## What this playbook does not do

It does not tell you whether equity compensation is worth accepting, and it does not value the award.
It tells you which questions decide the value, and which documents decide the tax.

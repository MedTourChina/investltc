## The distinction

China's foreign-exchange rules treat cross-border money as belonging to one of two categories:

- **Current account (经常项目)** — trade in goods and services, income, and current transfers.
  Salary, family support, consulting fees, tourism, profits from a business.
- **Capital account (资本项目)** — investment and financial flows. Securities, direct investment,
  loans, real estate purchases.

The category decides whether a transaction is routine or requires approval, and who is allowed to
do it.

## Why it matters for investing

Moving money for **spending or supporting yourself** sits in the current account. Moving money for
**investment** sits in the capital account. That is why:

- A foreign resident can receive a salary from abroad, convert it, and spend it.
- A foreign resident can invest **RMB already earned in China** through onshore accounts.
- A person abroad cannot simply wire funds in and buy shares — that would be a capital-account
  transaction, and individuals have no general channel for it.

## The comparison

| | Current account | Capital account |
|---|---|---|
| Typical transactions | Salary, services, family support, tourism | Securities, FDI, loans, property |
| Individual access | Yes, with documents and a quota | Essentially no, except specific schemes |
| Institutional access | Routine | Yes, through licensed routes |
| Regulator's posture | Facilitate | Manage, register, approve |
| Common misunderstanding | “The 50k quota lets me invest” | “If the bank accepts the wire, it must be allowed” |

## The trap

People assume that because a transfer executes, it is permitted. Banks process payments; they do not
always adjudicate purpose at the moment of transfer. A transaction can complete and still be
non-compliant, with consequences later — frozen accounts, clawbacks, or personal liability.

The safe rule: if you cannot name the regulation that permits the transaction, do not rely on the
fact that it went through.

## The short answer

There is no general legal route for an individual living outside China to wire money in and buy
securities. What exists is narrower:

- **Individuals** can bring money in for **current-account purposes** — salary, family support,
  selling a personal asset — within an annual conversion quota. That is not an investment channel.
- **Residents** (foreigners living in China) invest with **RMB they already hold here**, through
  onshore accounts, funds and brokerages.
- **Institutions** have real inbound investment routes: QFII, Northbound Stock Connect, the
  interbank bond market, QFLP and direct investment.

Most English guides blur these three together. That is why people arrive at a bank with the wrong
expectation and leave convinced the system is closed.

## Two doors, one border

Chinese foreign-exchange rules split flows into two categories, and the split decides everything.

| | Current account | Capital account |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | Salary, family support, services, tourism, profit remittance | Securities investment, direct investment, loans |
| Individuals | **Allowed** with a documented purpose, inside the annual facilitation quota | **Essentially closed** — no individual QFII |
| Institutions | — | **Allowed** through licensed routes, with registration and a custodian |
| Practical effect | Money can come in to be spent or saved | Money can come in to be invested, but only through a specific channel |

> The USD 50,000 figure you see everywhere is the **annual facilitation quota for conversion**
> under the current account. It is not an investment allowance, and it does not create a legal path
> into securities.

## What an individual can actually do

1. **Live in China and earn RMB.** Open a bank account, then a fund or brokerage account. This is
   the widest and most common route, and it needs no cross-border transfer at all.
2. **Receive money from abroad for a real current-account purpose.** Salary, family support, or the
   proceeds of selling a personal asset. Convert inside the quota, keep the documents.
3. **Use an institutional or offshore route.** Northbound Stock Connect through a Hong Kong broker
   is the main published path for non-residents. It is an offshore arrangement with onshore exposure.

What is **not** on the list: opening an A-share account from abroad, buying onshore funds as a
non-resident through a retail platform, or converting a large sum for "investment" at a bank counter.

## What institutions do

| Route | Who | What it gives | Main cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| QFII / RQFII | Licensed foreign institutions | Broad onshore securities access via a custodian | Compliance, custody, months of onboarding |
| Northbound Stock Connect | Investors via Hong Kong brokers | Eligible A-shares and ETFs | Broker eligibility, FX spread, withholding |
| CIBM Direct | Foreign institutions | Interbank bonds | Registration, custody, settlement setup |
| QFLP | Foreign capital into private equity | Onshore PE/VC funds | Local pilot approval |
| FDI | Foreign companies | Operating entities and capital injections | Registration, capital-account procedures |

Each of these is a project, not a form. Budget months, and expect the custodian to drive the process.

## What not to do

<div class="callout callout--danger">
<p class="callout__title">These are violations, not workarounds</p>
<p><strong>Splitting transfers</strong> across family and friends to stay under reporting thresholds.
<strong>Stating a false purpose</strong> on an FX declaration. <strong>Using underground banks</strong>
or informal value-transfer networks. Each carries real legal and financial risk, including account
freezing and personal liability, and none of them creates a defensible position if something goes wrong.</p>
</div>

If a route only works because nobody asked what the money was for, it is not a route.

## The realistic path, in order

1. **If you live here:** bank account → fund account → brokerage account, all in RMB. Start with the
   [bank account](/channels/bank-account) and [A-share](/channels/a-share) pages.
2. **If you are abroad:** decide whether you are an individual or an institution. Individuals should
   look at [Northbound Stock Connect](/channels/northbound); institutions at [QFII](/channels/qfii).
3. **If you are bringing money in:** confirm the purpose code with your bank before you send anything.
   Read the [USD 50,000 explainer](/explainers/usd-50k-quota) first.
4. **If someone offers a shortcut:** ask which regulation permits it. If the answer is vague, walk away.

## Questions to ask your bank

- Which purpose code applies to this transfer, and what documents support it?
- Is this a current-account or capital-account transaction?
- What happens if the receiving bank asks for more documents three months later?
- Can you show me the written rule, not the branch's summary of it?

## What this page is not

It is not legal or tax advice, and it is not an invitation to structure a transaction. Rules change,
and bank practice varies by branch and city. Confirm with the bank and, for anything material, with a
lawyer.

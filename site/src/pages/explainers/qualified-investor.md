## The rule

Private funds in China can only be sold to **qualified investors**. The standard thresholds are:

- **Financial assets** of at least **CNY 3 million**, or average annual personal income of at least
  **CNY 500,000** over the past three years; and
- a minimum subscription of **CNY 1 million** per private fund.

Institutions face their own test, based on net assets.

These numbers are set by the private-fund rules and can be adjusted; treat them as the current
benchmark rather than a permanent constant.

## Why the gate exists

Private funds are less liquid, less transparent and less protected than public funds. The regulator's
logic is that only investors who can absorb a loss and lock up capital for years should be allowed in.
The gate is a consumer-protection device, not a status symbol.

## What it means in practice

- A foreign resident with a normal salary and savings is, in most cases, **not** a qualified investor.
  The public fund shelf is the relevant one.
- Meeting the threshold does not make a private fund suitable. It only makes it legal to offer.
- The CNY 1 million minimum is per fund, not per year.

## The common mistake

People read the income threshold and assume they qualify on salary alone. The rule usually requires
**financial assets** at the higher number, or income averaged over three years. Documentation is
required; a bank statement showing one good year is not enough.

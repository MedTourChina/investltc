## What the account is

The personal pension account (个人养老金) is not a product. It is a wrapper: a bank-held account with a
contribution cap, a tax rule and a lock. Inside it you choose pension savings deposits, pension
wealth-management products, pension target-date funds or pension insurance. The wrapper is what the
state is subsidising; the products inside are ordinary products you could otherwise buy with no lock.

Three numbers define it:

| Number | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| CNY 12,000 | The most you can put in per calendar year. It does not roll over. |
| 3% | The rate applied to the whole balance when you take it out. |
| 0 | The times you can touch it before retirement age, unless you lose working capacity, settle abroad, or die. |

## The tax arithmetic, without the brochure

You deduct the contribution from taxable income in the year you make it, the investments grow
untaxed inside the account, and the whole balance is taxed at 3% on the way out.

So the account pays if your marginal rate today is above 3%, and it costs you if it is not. Someone
in the 3% bracket gets a lock-up and an account to manage in exchange for nothing. Someone at 20% or
45% gets the difference between their rate and 3% on CNY 12,000 a year — real, but capped by the
annual limit, and only visible if they actually claim the deduction.

The deduction is claimed in the **annual tax settlement**, not through payroll. If you never file, you
never got the benefit and you still took the lock.

## The enrolment gate

The account requires that you are already enrolled in China's basic pension insurance (基本养老保险).
That is the gate, and it is not a passport question. An employed foreign national is normally enrolled
with everyone else; nationals of countries with a bilateral social-security agreement are often exempt
from the pension contribution — and if you are exempt, you are not enrolled, and this account is not
open to you.

So the honest sequence is:

1. Check your social-insurance record (社保缴费记录) from the local office or the 12333 app.
2. If it shows pension contributions, the account is available at a bank.
3. If it does not, the account is closed to you however long your residence permit runs.

This is the same pattern as the rest of this site: the document is not the eligibility, the record is.

## Leaving China, and the exit

Settling abroad (出国定居) is one of the legal withdrawal triggers, together with reaching retirement
age, permanent loss of working capacity, and death. In practice that means: documents proving you are
leaving and cancelling your household registration here — and the operator at the counter may not have
done one before.

If your plan is to leave in two years, the relevant fact is not the tax spread. It is that the money
is unavailable until the paperwork for departure is complete, and that paperwork is the same paperwork
you are already doing to close accounts. Read [Leaving China](/playbooks/leaving-china) before you
contribute in your last year.

## Where it sits in the map

- It is an **onshore, RMB, resident** channel, and only for someone already inside the social-insurance system.
- It is not a route for money from abroad. The contribution has to be your own onshore RMB income.
- It is not a substitute for a deposit: nothing inside is covered by deposit insurance unless the
  product itself is a deposit.
- It is not a way to keep money working after you leave. The lock and the exit trigger are the point.

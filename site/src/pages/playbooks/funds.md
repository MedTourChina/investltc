## Why funds first

A public fund account is usually easier to open than a brokerage account, and it gives you a much
wider shelf: money-market funds, bond funds, equity funds and index funds. For most foreign
residents this is the sensible first investment account, not the last.

## Three ways in

| Channel | What it is good for | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| Bank | Face-to-face help, familiar if you already bank there | Narrower shelf, higher fees, branch-dependent rules |
| Broker | Wider shelf, one account for funds and shares | Account opening friction is higher |
| Platform | Best prices and fund range | Online onboarding often assumes a Chinese ID |

Start with the channel where you already have a relationship. A bank that knows you will open a fund
account in twenty minutes; a platform that has never seen a passport may take a week.

## The onboarding step that breaks

The failure point is almost always identity verification, not the investment itself:

- The app's "open account" flow asks for a Chinese ID number.
- The passport name has a space, a middle name, or a hyphen the system does not accept.
- The bank card is not yet linked for fund settlement.

If the online flow fails, do not keep retrying. Ask the branch to open the fund account at the counter,
then link the card.

## Test the plumbing with a money-market fund

Before you buy an equity fund, buy CNY 100 of a money-market fund. You are testing four things:

1. Can money leave your bank account?
2. Does the fund account show the holding?
3. How long does a redemption take to come back?
4. Are the fees what the screen said?

If any of those fail, you want to find out with CNY 100, not CNY 100,000.

## Fees, in the order they matter

1. **Redemption fee** — often 1.5% if you sell within seven days. This is the one that catches people.
2. **Management fee** — 0.15–1.5% a year, taken inside the NAV, so you never see a bill.
3. **Subscription fee** — 0.1–1.5%, usually discounted on platforms.
4. **Custody and other fees** — small, but read them.

Two share classes of the same fund can differ mainly in fees. Compare the same fund's A and C classes
before choosing.

## Redemption timing

Money-market funds are usually T+0 or T+1. Bond and equity funds are typically T+1 to T+7, depending
on the fund and the channel. Plan around settlement, not around the price you saw.

## Tax

Fund distributions and gains are generally treated as investment income, with different treatment for
residents and non-residents. Do not infer your position from a blog post — including this one.

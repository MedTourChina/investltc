## What withholding means

Most investment income in China is taxed **at source**. The bank, broker or fund deducts the tax
before paying you, so what lands in your account is already net. You rarely file anything; you simply
receive less than the gross amount.

That is why a dividend yield quoted in a brochure and the cash you receive can differ, and why “do I
need to declare this?” has a different answer from “was tax already paid?”.

## The rough map

| Income | Typical treatment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bank deposit interest | Exempt for individuals since 2008 | Applies to ordinary deposits |
| Savings treasury bond interest | Exempt for individuals | Confirm for your instrument |
| Corporate bond interest | Taxable, withheld at source | Rate depends on your status and the issuer |
| A-share dividends | Withheld, with holding-period rules | Rates differ for residents and non-residents |
| A-share capital gains | Individuals have long been exempt | Do not assume this survives a change of status |
| Fund distributions and redemptions | Partly exempt, partly taxed | Depends on the fund type and your status |
| Northbound dividends | Withheld before payment | Treaty relief may be available |

## Resident versus non-resident

The same dividend can be taxed differently depending on whether you are a Chinese tax resident. That
is why the [183-day question](/explainers/tax-residency-183-days) comes before the withholding
question.

For non-residents, the statutory rate on dividends, interest and similar income is generally 20%,
often reduced by a tax treaty to 10% or lower — but the reduced rate usually requires paperwork, and
some payers apply the default rate and leave you to claim the difference.

## The part people miss

1. **Withholding is not the end of your obligation.** Your home country may tax the same income and
   give you a credit for Chinese tax paid. Without a withholding certificate, the credit is hard to claim.
2. **CRS means it is not invisible.** Account and income information is exchanged between tax
   authorities. A Chinese broker reporting your account is normal, not alarming.
3. **Treaty relief needs a form.** If you want the treaty rate rather than the default, ask the payer
   which certificate they need — usually a tax residency certificate from your home country.

## What to keep

- Annual statements from each bank, broker and fund platform.
- Withholding certificates (完税证明) where the payer issues them.
- Records of when you bought and sold, not just the totals.
- Your day count, if your residency status changed during the year.

## What this page is not

It is not tax advice. Rates, exemptions and treaty positions change, and the answer depends on your
nationality, your residency and the instrument. Treat the table as a map of the questions to ask, not
as an answer.

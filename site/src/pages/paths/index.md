## Why the path matters more than the product

The [first thirty days](/start) is a generic sequence. Your status changes it: which steps you can
skip, which ones refuse you, and which documents you will be asked for.

Four paths cover almost everyone. Whichever one you are on, the [eligibility check](/check) shows
that path's next three steps above the results, because it reads the same visa answer you gave it.

| Path | On this site |
| --- | --- |
| **Student** | Bank account yes, brokerage usually no |
| **Work permit** | The full sequence, including tax and the housing fund |
| **Permanent residence** | Faster at every counter, but no extra investment rights |
| **Dependant** | Bank account yes, everything else depends on the working spouse |

If you are from Hong Kong, Macao or Taiwan, the identification documents differ and some of this does
not apply — check locally rather than mapping your case onto these four.

## The one thing all four share

**Status never unlocks an investment channel that the rules do not provide.** A residence permit, a
work permit and permanent residence all make the *administrative* steps easier. None of them changes
what a foreign individual may legally buy, which is why the [matrix](/matrix) looks the same for
everyone in the individual columns.

## If you are not sure which one you are

Your residence permit states the purpose. The words to look for:

- **学习 / 学习类** — student, see [student](/paths/student).
- **工作 / 工作类** — employment, see [work permit](/paths/work-permit).
- **永久居留** — permanent residence, see [permanent residence](/paths/permanent-residence).
- **团聚 / 私人事务** — family reunion or personal affairs, see [dependant](/paths/dependant).

When two apply, the more restricted one usually governs what a bank or broker will accept.

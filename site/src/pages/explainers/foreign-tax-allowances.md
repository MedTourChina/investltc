## The choice

A foreign employee in China has two ways to reduce taxable salary, and must pick one **per tax year**:

| | **Eight allowances (津补贴免税)** | **Special additional deductions (专项附加扣除)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open to | Foreign individuals | Everyone |
| Method | Reimbursed expenses, exempt from tax | Fixed monthly amounts deducted from income |
| Needs receipts | **Yes** — reimbursed, not paid as cash | No |
| Covers | Housing, meals, relocation, laundry, children's schooling, language training, travel, home leave | Children's education, infant care, further education, housing loan interest, rent, elderly care, serious illness |
| Flexibility | Depends on your employer's payroll | Claimed by you in the app |

The rule is the important part: **choose one, not both**, and the choice is fixed for that tax year.
This is the single most common omission in a foreign employee's first Chinese tax return.

The policy has been extended repeatedly and currently runs **through 2027** — check before relying on
it for a later year.

## Why the allowance route often wins

The standard deductions have monthly ceilings: children's education around CNY 2,000 per child, rent
between CNY 800 and 1,500 depending on the city, elderly care up to CNY 3,000.

The allowance route has no such ceiling — it exempts the **actual, reasonable expense**. If your
employer pays international school fees of CNY 200,000 a year, the allowance route can exempt far
more than the fixed child-education deduction. That is why the choice matters most for people with
children at international schools or with expensive housing.

## The conditions that kill it

The exemption is for **reimbursed, non-cash** benefits. It fails if:

- The allowance is **paid as cash** in your salary. Cash allowances are taxable salary.
- There are **no invoices** in your name, or the invoices are for something outside the list.
- The amount is **unreasonable** — the standard is "reasonable", and a housing allowance far above
  market rent invites a question.
- You claimed the **special deductions as well**. The app will happily let you; the reconciliation
  will claw it back.

## The eight, briefly

1. **Housing** — rent paid, reimbursed against the lease and invoices.
2. **Meals** — a food allowance, reimbursed.
3. **Relocation** — moving to or from China.
4. **Laundry** — the one everybody forgets.
5. **Children's education** — school fees, in China.
6. **Language training** — Chinese lessons.
7. **Business travel** — domestic and overseas, within the employer's standard.
8. **Home leave** — trips to visit family, conventionally up to twice a year.

## How to decide

1. Ask payroll **which route the company already operates** — most have one, because it has to
   withhold correctly.
2. Add up what you actually spend on the eight categories, with invoices.
3. Compare that total with the fixed deductions you could claim.
4. Choose once, in writing, and keep the vouchers for five years.

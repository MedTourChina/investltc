## The short version

An **NRA (Non-Resident Account)** is an onshore account held by a non-resident entity or person. It
lets money sit inside China without becoming domestic money in the full sense: it is a foreign-currency
account with defined uses, opened by a bank for a customer whose residence is outside the mainland.

For individuals, banks do not always use the term precisely. A foreigner who does not live in China
may be offered an “NRA”, a 境外个人账户, or simply declined. The label matters less than the account's
**permitted uses**, which are narrower than a resident account's.

## Who uses it

| Holder | Typical use |
|---|---|
| Foreign company | Receiving payments from Chinese customers, paying local expenses, holding FX |
| Foreign individual | Receiving funds from abroad, limited local payments |
| Foreign bank | Correspondent and clearing relationships |

## What it can do

- Hold foreign currency onshore.
- Receive payments related to current-account transactions.
- Convert to RMB within the applicable rules and quota.

## What it cannot do — or does only with approval

- Act as a full domestic settlement account for investments.
- Buy securities without a separate, specific channel.
- Substitute for the capital-account approvals that investment flows require.

<div class="callout callout--warn">
<p class="callout__title">“I have an NRA, so I can invest”</p>
<p>Not necessarily. An NRA is a payments account, not an investment licence. Whether you can buy a
fund or a wealth product with it depends on the product and the bank, and in many cases the answer
is no.</p>
</div>

## Practical questions to ask

- Is this an NRA, a 境外个人账户, or a domestic account?
- What purposes can this account be used for, in writing?
- Can it receive payments from a Chinese company?
- What is the minimum balance, and what are the monthly fees?
- What documents are needed to close it from abroad?

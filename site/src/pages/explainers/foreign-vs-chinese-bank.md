## The short answer

Open **both** if you are staying more than a year, and in this order:

1. A **Chinese bank** first, because payroll, Alipay, WeChat Pay, the housing fund and social insurance
   all assume a domestic card.
2. A **foreign bank** second, if you need multi-currency accounts, English service, or a relationship
   that travels with you.

Neither is better. They solve different problems.

## What is the same

- **Deposit insurance.** CNY 500,000 per depositor per bank, and it covers foreign banks operating in
  China as well as Chinese ones. The logo on the door does not change the ceiling.
- **The rules.** Foreign-exchange policy, the USD 50,000 conversion quota and the purpose tests apply
  to every bank in the country. A foreign bank cannot get you around them.
- **The identification.** Passport, residence permit, and increasingly a face check at the counter.

## What differs

| | Chinese bank | Foreign bank |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Branches | Everywhere | A handful of cities |
| Language | Chinese, sometimes English at large branches | English by default |
| Opening an account | Usually in person, documents inspected closely | In person, often by appointment |
| Minimum balance | Often none | Often a daily-average threshold, with a monthly fee below it |
| Product shelf | Wide: funds, wealth products, bonds, gold | Narrower, and skewed to deposits and funds |
| Multi-currency | Possible but not their focus | Their focus |
| Speed of decisions | Local, immediate | Can route through a regional hub |

## The account tiers that catch people out

Chinese banks classify accounts, and the class decides how much money can move:

- **Class I (一类户)** — full function, opened in person with documents. This is the one you want.
- **Class II (二类户)** — limited: roughly CNY 10,000 a day in and out on the unbound side, and an
  annual cap. Fine as a second account, useless as your salary account.
- **Class III (三类户)** — a payment wallet, with a balance cap. Not for savings.

If an account is opened quickly online or by a clerk in a hurry, check which class you were given.
A salary landing in a Class II account will hit the cap.

## The multi-currency question

A Chinese bank can hold USD, EUR or HKD for you. What it cannot do is let you treat that balance as
offshore money: converting it to renminbi is an FX transaction with a purpose requirement, and moving
it out is a cross-border transfer.

That is the real reason people keep a foreign bank account: not better service, but a second legal
place for the money to sit. See [CNY and CNH](/explainers/cny-vs-cnh).

## Practical sequence

1. Bring passport, residence permit, employment contract, and a local address proof.
2. Ask for a **Class I** account, and ask what your daily transfer limit is.
3. Bind it to Alipay and WeChat Pay the same week — most daily life assumes it.
4. Ask payroll which bank they prefer before you choose, because salary rails are bank-specific.
5. If you also want a foreign bank, book an appointment and ask about the minimum balance first.

## Why this is step one

Almost every onshore channel ends in a Chinese bank account. Funds settle into it, a brokerage links
it as the third-party depository, and salary arrives in it. Everything downstream is easier if the
account is opened at a branch that has done it before.

## Before you go

Two decisions matter more than the bank's brand:

1. **Which branch.** A branch near a university or an international business district sees foreign
   passports weekly. A suburban branch may see one a year.
2. **Which bank.** The big four (ICBC, CCB, BOC, ABC) have the widest coverage; the joint-stock banks
   (CMB, SPDB, CITIC) tend to have better apps and English support.

Ask one question on the phone before you travel: *"Do you open accounts for foreign passport holders
at this branch, and what documents do you need?"* If the answer is vague, try the next branch. The
rule is national; the experience is local.

## What to bring

- [ ] Passport, valid for at least six months
- [ ] Visa or residence permit
- [ ] Chinese mobile number registered in your own name
- [ ] Proof of address: lease, utility bill, or the police registration form
- [ ] Employment letter or student certificate (not always required, often asked for)
- [ ] A small amount of cash or an existing card, if you want to deposit on the day

## At the counter

Expect 40–90 minutes. The sequence is usually:

1. Account opening form — name exactly as in the passport.
2. **Tax residency self-certification.** This is the CRS form. You declare where you are tax
   resident. Answer honestly; it is shared with tax authorities under CRS.
3. Identity verification, photo, signature specimens.
4. Choosing the account type: a basic settlement account is what you want.
5. Setting the app password and enabling mobile banking.

Ask for the account number and confirm the app shows the balance before you leave. A common failure
is an account that exists but cannot receive transfers.

## What they will ask

- Why do you need the account? ("Salary and living expenses" is the normal answer.)
- Where do you live? Have the address document ready.
- Are you tax resident in China? The 183-day test decides this; see the
  [residency explainer](/explainers/tax-residency-183-days).

## If you are refused

| What they say | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| “We don't do foreign passports” | The branch has no experience | Ask for the manager, or try another branch |
| “You need a Chinese ID” | Staff applying an online rule to a counter case | Ask them to process it manually |
| “Your address proof is not enough” | The document has no address or no name | Bring the police registration form |
| “Come back with a work permit” | Branch-level policy | Try another branch of the same bank |

Do not argue about the rule. Ask which document would satisfy them and move on. A refusal is a branch
event, not a bank policy.

## After it is open

- Enable the app and test a small transfer both ways.
- Check the daily transfer limit; some accounts are opened with a low default.
- Add the card to Alipay or WeChat Pay if you want to pay with your phone.
- Keep the account active: dormant accounts can be restricted, and reactivating from abroad is painful.

## Costs

Account fees are usually zero to CNY 10 a month. Domestic transfers are free or a couple of yuan.
International wires out cost CNY 100–300 plus correspondent fees.

## If you leave China

You can usually keep the account, but it may be restricted if it goes dormant, and some banks require
an in-person visit to close it. Decide before you leave, not after — see the
[inbound funding playbook](/playbooks/inbound-funding) for why the direction of money matters.

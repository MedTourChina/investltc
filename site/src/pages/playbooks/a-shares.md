## Who qualifies

Foreign nationals **working or studying in mainland China** can open an A-share account. The
published basis is the 2018 ChinaClear notice allowing foreign individuals with a residence permit
to open securities accounts. In practice you need:

- a passport,
- a valid residence permit,
- proof of employment (or proof of study),
- a local bank card for the settlement account.

If you are here as a tourist, on a short business trip, or living abroad, this route does not apply.
See [Northbound Stock Connect](/channels/northbound) instead.

## Before you go: pick the branch, not the brand

The single biggest predictor of success is whether the branch has done this before. Brokers apply
the same national rule very differently.

1. Shortlist three brokers: one large state-owned, one mid-size, one foreign-invested.
2. Call each and ask exactly one question: *"Do you open A-share accounts for foreign passport
   holders at this branch, and what documents do you need?"*
3. Ask for the name of the person you spoke to, and the branch address.
4. Book an appointment. Walk-ins are frequently turned away.

## What to bring

- [ ] Passport, valid for at least six months
- [ ] Residence permit
- [ ] Employment letter stating your position and contract term (or student certificate)
- [ ] Local bank card, already activated
- [ ] Chinese mobile number, registered in your name
- [ ] Proof of address, if the branch asks
- [ ] The branch contact's name and extension

## At the branch

Expect 60–120 minutes. The sequence is usually:

1. Account opening form and the investor suitability questionnaire.
2. Identity verification and a photo.
3. Signature specimens and the risk disclosure.
4. Linking the bank card as the third-party depository (三方存管).
5. Setting the trading password and enabling the app.

Ask for the account number and confirm that the settlement account is linked before you leave. A
common failure is an account that exists but cannot receive money.

## If you are refused

Refusals are usually one of four things, and each has a response:

| What they say | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| “We don't do foreign accounts” | This branch has not been trained | Ask for the branch manager, or try another branch |
| “You need a Chinese ID” | Staff are applying the online onboarding rule to a counter case | Ask them to process it manually at the counter |
| “Your employment letter is not enough” | The letter lacks position or term | Get a new letter with both, stamped |
| “The system won't accept your passport” | Name formatting or document type mismatch | Ask them to check the document type code, not the number |

Do not argue about the rule. Ask which document would satisfy them, in writing if possible, and move
to the next branch. A refusal is a branch event, not a legal fact.

## After it is open

- Fund the settlement account from your own bank account, not from a third party.
- Buy one lot (100 shares) as a test of the settlement path before committing more.
- Check the commission you were given — the default is rarely the best rate, and it is negotiable.
- Set the app to English if available; many brokers' apps are Chinese-only.

## Costs and taxes

- Commission: typically 0.01–0.03% per trade, often with a CNY 5 minimum.
- Stamp duty: 0.05% on sales (buy side exempt).
- Transfer fee: 0.001% of turnover.

Dividends and capital gains are taxed differently for residents and non-residents, and the treatment
can depend on your home country's treaty. This is the one part of the process where a tax adviser
earns their fee.

## Getting out

Sell, then transfer the cash from the securities account back to the linked bank account. Settlement
is T+1 for shares. Closing the account entirely usually requires a branch visit.

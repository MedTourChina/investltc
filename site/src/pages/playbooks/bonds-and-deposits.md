## Why start here

Most English-language coverage of investing in China jumps to equities. That is the wrong order. The
channels that a foreign resident can open quickly, fund legally from RMB income, and exit without a
counterparty problem are the boring ones: deposits, savings bonds, exchange-traded government bonds,
and money-market funds.

If you are here for a year or two, this is probably the whole of your onshore portfolio. That is not a
failure of ambition. It is the shape of the access.

## The shelf, in order of friction

| Product | Minimum | Where you buy it | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bank deposit | CNY 50–100 | Any branch or app | Covered by deposit insurance to CNY 500,000 per bank |
| Savings bonds (储蓄国债) | CNY 100 | Bank counter, on issue days | Fixed term, usually 3 or 5 years, sold in windows |
| Exchange government bonds | CNY 1,000 face | Brokerage account | Tradable, but retail lot sizes and thin books |
| Money-market fund | CNY 1–100 | Bank, broker or fund app | T+0 or T+1 redemption, yields track short rates |
| Bank wealth product | CNY 10,000+ | Branch or app | Not a deposit; the risk grade is the gate |

Read that table as a ladder of effort, not of return. The further down you go, the more paperwork
stands between you and the product.

## Deposits: the part everyone skips

A CNY deposit at a big-four bank pays a published rate that is usually below inflation. A small or
city commercial bank may pay more, because it needs funding. The trade-off is real and simple: the
deposit insurance scheme covers CNY 500,000 per depositor per bank, so amounts above that at one
institution are not insured.

Two practical points:

1. **Term deposits are priced by tenor.** Three months to five years, with the rate stepping up. Early
   withdrawal usually pays the demand-deposit rate, which is close to zero — so do not lock money you
   may need.
2. **Foreign passport holders can open them.** The gate is the bank account, not the product. Some
   branches will try to sell you a wealth product instead; decline if you want a deposit.

## Savings bonds

Treasury savings bonds are sold to individuals in scheduled windows, usually in March to November, and
often sell out on the first morning at popular branches. They are bought at the counter, in person, with
your bank account and ID. Interest is paid periodically or at maturity depending on the series, and
early redemption is possible with a penalty.

The catch is availability and timing, not eligibility. Ask the branch which issue is coming and when
the window opens; the app usually does not list them.

## Exchange bonds

Once you have a brokerage account, you can buy government, policy-bank and listed corporate bonds on
the Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges. Three things decide whether it works:

- **Lot size.** Bonds trade in lots of ten units, CNY 1,000 of face value each, so a single purchase is
  CNY 10,000 of face value before price.
- **Suitability.** Corporate bonds are split between ordinary and qualified investors. A retail
  account does not get everything, and the split is enforced by the broker.
- **Liquidity.** A quoted price is not a market. Small issues may not trade for days, and the bid-ask
  spread can eat a year of coupon.

For a foreign resident the practical route is government and policy-bank paper, held to maturity.

## Tax, in one paragraph

Interest income is generally subject to individual income tax, withheld at source by the payer.
Government bond interest has specific exemptions; corporate bond coupons usually do not. Capital gains
on exchange bonds depend on the instrument and your status. The withholding explainer covers the
mechanics; this is an area where you should confirm your own position rather than assume.

## What to do this week

1. Check whether your bank offers savings bonds and when the next issue opens.
2. If you already have a brokerage account, ask whether bond permissions are enabled.
3. Move anything above CNY 500,000 out of a single bank, or accept that it is uninsured.

None of this is exciting. All of it is open to you, which is more than can be said for most of the
rest of the map.

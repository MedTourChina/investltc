## The same fund, two fee structures

Most onshore funds offer two share classes of the same portfolio:

- **A class**: you pay a subscription fee up front (often 1.5%, discounted to 0.1–0.15% on platforms),
  and no sales service fee afterwards.
- **C class**: no subscription fee, but a sales service fee of roughly 0.2–0.8% a year, charged inside
  the fund's net asset value.

The portfolio is identical. The difference is purely when you pay.

## The breakeven

Ignoring discounts, with a 1.5% front load and a 0.4% annual service fee, C class overtakes A class after
about **3.5 years**. With the platform discount to 0.1%, the breakeven moves out to roughly **a few
months**, because the front load is nearly free.

So the rule of thumb:

| Holding period | Usually cheaper |
| --- | --- |
| Under a year | C class |
| One to three years | Depends on the discount you actually get |
| Over three years | A class |

The only number that matters is the discounted subscription fee your platform charges, not the headline
1.5%. Check it before choosing the class.

## Where the fees hide

- **Redemption fee.** Often 0.5% below seven days, 0.75% below thirty, zero after. Short holding periods
  are penalised.
- **Management and custody fees.** Charged daily inside the net asset value, identical across classes.
- **Sales service fee.** C class only, also inside the net asset value — you never see a bill for it.

This is why a fund's quoted return is net of fees and why comparing two funds on quoted return compares
their after-fee outcomes, not their skill.

## What to do at the counter

1. Ask which classes the fund offers, and the **discounted** subscription fee for A class.
2. Ask the sales service fee for C class.
3. Divide: front load ÷ annual service fee = breakeven years. Compare it to your horizon.
4. Check the redemption fee schedule for the period you might sell in.

A fund salesperson may push C class because it pays a trailing fee. That does not make it wrong for a
short holding period — but it does mean the recommendation is not disinterested. Do the division
yourself.
